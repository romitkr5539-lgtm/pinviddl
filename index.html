How to Download a Pinterest Video

1

Open the Pin on Pinterest and copy its link from the share menu or address bar.

2

Paste the copied link into the PinViddl input box and click Download.

3

PinViddl finds the video. Click the download link to save it to your device.

What happens after you paste a Pin link

PinViddl is a Pinterest video downloader that works from a Pin's link. You give it the address of one public Pin, it looks for the media attached to that Pin, and it offers the file for download. It never asks for your Pinterest login and it doesn't touch your boards. That is the difference from Pinterest's own Save button, which puts a Pin on one of your boards instead of putting a file on your device.

When you press Download, your browser first checks that the text looks like a Pinterest Pin address. If it doesn't, a short message appears straight away and nothing is sent. If it does, the link goes to PinViddl's server, which runs on Cloudflare Workers, and the server looks the Pin up and returns whatever media it found. You then see a preview player and one row for each file, with a Download button and a Copy URL button. Copy URL copies that file's direct address, not the Pin link.

Only public Pins can be fetched. If a Pin is private or has been deleted, there is nothing for the tool to retrieve.

Which Pinterest links work

Pinterest has several kinds of address, and only some of them lead to one specific video. PinViddl accepts two:

  • A Pin link, such as https://www.pinterest.com/pin/1234567890/. The same /pin/ format is accepted on several Pinterest country domains.
  • A pin.it short link, such as https://pin.it/abc123, the shortened address Pinterest creates when you share a Pin.

Links to a board, a profile, a search results page or your home feed show many Pins at once, so there is no single video to fetch and they are rejected.

Paste the whole address, including the https:// at the start. Links copied from the Pinterest app or a browser's address bar already include it. A link typed by hand without it will be refused. If you're unsure what you copied, open it in a new tab: if it lands on one Pin with its own page, it's the right kind of link.

Saving the file on your phone or computer

PinViddl runs in the browser, so where the video ends up depends on your browser and device settings rather than on PinViddl.

  • Android: in the Pinterest app, open the Pin, tap Share and choose Copy link (the labels shift a little between app versions). Paste it into PinViddl in your browser. The file usually lands in the Downloads folder, which you can open from a file manager.
  • iPhone: the steps are the same. Safari normally stores downloads in the Files app under Downloads. To get the video into Photos, open it from Files, tap Share and choose Save Video.
  • Windows and Mac: copy the link from the address bar or the Pin's share menu. The file goes to your browser's usual downloads folder, or you get a save prompt if your browser is set to ask.

Video quality and file type

PinViddl has no resolution setting of its own. It lists the video file or files it found for the Pin, and each row shows a quality label when one is available. If a Pin comes in more than one version you will see more than one row; if it has one version, you will see one.

What you get is limited by what the uploader provided and what Pinterest serves for that Pin, so a small, low-resolution clip will not become sharper by being downloaded. After saving, check the real resolution in your device's file details or a video player's info panel. Video Pins normally arrive as MP4 files, but the format is whatever Pinterest provides for that Pin.

Some videos have a logo or caption burned into the picture by the creator. That is part of the video itself, and PinViddl cannot remove it.

If a Pin won't download

Most failures come down to the link or the Pin, and the message on screen usually tells you which:

  • A message asking for a valid Pinterest URL. The text isn't a Pin or pin.it address. It may be a board, profile or search link, a half-copied address, or one missing https://.
  • A message about the Pin being public. The Pin may be private or deleted, or the lookup failed for a moment. Open the link in a private or logged-out browser window. If Pinterest can't show you the Pin there, PinViddl most likely can't read it either.
  • A message that no downloadable media was found. The lookup returned no file for that Pin. Check on Pinterest that it really contains a video and not a photo or a looping animation.
  • The preview is blank but the Download button is there. The preview and the download are separate requests, so one can fail while the other works. Try the Download button anyway.
  • A Pin that worked before now fails. Pinterest can change how it delivers media. If a public Pin keeps failing, send the link through the contact page so it can be looked at.

Video, image or GIF?

Pins look alike in the feed but hold different things. A video Pin has playback controls. A short loop that plays by itself with no controls is often a GIF-style animation. A still picture is an image. What you receive depends on what the Pin actually contains, so PinViddl has a separate page for each:

Pinterest Image Downloader For still photos and graphics.
Pinterest GIF Downloader For looping animated Pins.

For ordinary image Pins, Pinterest also has its own Download image option in the Pin's ⋯ menu. Its help page notes that not every Pin can be downloaded that way.

Using downloaded videos responsibly

A Pin being public does not make its video free to reuse. The rights stay with whoever made or published it. Saving a clip to watch offline is a different thing from reposting it, editing it into your own work or using it commercially, and for those you should ask the creator first. What is allowed also depends on where you live and on Pinterest's own terms, neither of which PinViddl controls.
