How to Download an Image from Pinterest

  1. Open the Pin. Find the Pin with the photo you want, either in the Pinterest app or in your browser.
  2. Copy the Pin's link. Tap the share icon and choose "Copy Link," or copy it straight from your browser's address bar.
  3. Open PinViddl. Head to the Pinterest Image Downloader page.
  4. Paste the link. Drop the URL into the input box.
  5. Start the process. Give it a moment to load the image.
  6. Save it. Once the image appears, download it to your device.

If you can't find "Copy Link," look for the share icon on the Pin itself — Pinterest usually tucks it just below or beside the image.

Pinterest Pins and Photos: What's the Difference?

A Pinterest Pin is the individual post — it's the container. Inside sits the actual image, along with a title, a description, and usually a link back to wherever the image originally came from. The Pin page itself, with its comments and related Pins, is just the wrapper around that image.

When someone says they want to "download a Pin," what they usually mean is the image inside it, not the surrounding page. That's why PinViddl works from the Pin URL itself rather than asking you to dig up a direct image file link. Pinterest doesn't make those easy to find, and most people wouldn't want to hunt for one anyway.

Some Pins also live inside a board with dozens of other saved images. PinViddl works with the one Pin URL you give it — it isn't built to pull every image from an entire board at once.

Where Pinterest Images Come From and Why Saving Them Is Awkward

Pinterest is primarily a discovery platform — images arrive from blogs, e-commerce sites, design portfolios, and individual creators, all funnelled into a single feed. The image you want to save might have originated anywhere on the internet, but on Pinterest it sits inside a Pin wrapper with its own URL, title, and description.

Right-clicking and choosing "Save image as" on Pinterest frequently saves a low-resolution thumbnail rather than the full image, because Pinterest serves optimised previews for speed. PinViddl bypasses that by reading the Pin's actual source data and returning the highest-resolution version attached to that specific Pin — the same one a user would see if they clicked through to the original source.

Saving Pinterest Images to Your Phone's Camera Roll

On iPhone, once the image appears in PinViddl after processing, tap and hold it to get the context menu, then choose Add to Photos or Save to Files depending on where you want it. Safari on iOS can be inconsistent about this — if holding doesn't offer a save option, tap the share icon at the bottom of the browser and look for Save Image from there.

On Android, Chrome typically shows a download icon directly on the image, or you can long-press it and choose Download image. The file goes to your Downloads folder and usually appears in your gallery automatically. If it doesn't show up, open your Files app and check Downloads.

Image Resolution — What Determines the File Size You Get

Three things affect the resolution of the image PinViddl retrieves: what the original creator uploaded, what Pinterest chose to store (Pinterest sometimes resamples images on ingest), and what the Pin page exposes in its source data.

Wallpapers and photography Pins tend to have larger originals because those audiences care about print and screen quality. Recipe photos, quick DIY graphics, and meme-style content are often uploaded at smaller sizes to begin with. PinViddl returns whatever the Pin is serving — it doesn't estimate, reconstruct, or AI-upscale the image. If you need a print-quality version of something you found on Pinterest, the best route is always to follow the source link on the Pin back to the original website.

This Tool Is for Images — Here's Where Videos and GIFs Go

Pinterest hosts three media types, and each one behaves differently under the hood. Mixing them up in a single downloader produces poor results, which is why PinViddl separates them.

If the Pin plays when you tap it and has a progress bar, it's a video — use the Pinterest video downloader instead. Video Pins store their content as MP4 streams, not image files, so this page won't find anything useful in them.

If the Pin loops continuously without sound and there's no scrubbing control, it's animated content — usually stored as either a true GIF or an MP4 loop. The Pinterest GIF downloader handles both of those cases and explains the format difference in more detail.

When the Image Doesn't Show Up

A few things tend to cause trouble, and most have simple fixes:

  • You pasted a board link, not a Pin link. Board URLs look like pinterest.com/username/board-name/. Pin URLs contain /pin/ followed by a numeric ID. Open the specific Pin you want, not the board it belongs to, and copy that address.
  • The Pin is set to private. Private boards and secret Pins aren't accessible to anyone without a Pinterest login — PinViddl can't retrieve content that isn't publicly viewable.
  • The Pin was deleted or the source was taken down. Broken Pins on Pinterest show a blank or error state. If Pinterest itself can't display the image, there's nothing for PinViddl to find.
  • The link was cut off or copied partially. This happens when copying from certain mobile apps. Go back to the Pin and re-copy the full URL before trying again.

Downloading Images Responsibly

Saving a Pinterest image for your own personal reference — a recipe to cook later, a wallpaper for your home screen, a design reference for a project — is a straightforward personal use case. Where it gets more complicated is when that image goes back onto the internet.

Most Pinterest images are protected by copyright. The creator who took the photo or designed the graphic holds those rights, and Pinterest's terms don't transfer them to you when you view or save a Pin. Reposting without credit, using images in commercial work, or building a product around someone else's photography without permission are all potential infringement issues. When in doubt, link back to the source or contact the creator directly.
